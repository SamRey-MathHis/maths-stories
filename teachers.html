For Teachers

Ideas for using the history of maths to spark curiosity in your classroom.

Why teach with history?

Students often ask, "Why do we need to learn this?" Stories from the history of mathematics show that every concept was invented to solve a real problem, by real people, in many different cultures. Hearing the story first can give students a reason to care about the method.

Using a story in a lesson

  1. Open with the problem (5 minutes)

    Before playing the story, pose the historical problem to the class. For negative numbers: "A merchant owes more than they own. How do you write that down?"

  2. Play the story (5 to 15 minutes)

    Play one chapter on the classroom screen with the sound on. Each chapter is short enough to fit in a single lesson.

  3. Discuss the reasoning (10 minutes)

    Ask how the people in the story thought about the problem. What would the class have done in their place?

  4. Try it yourself (10 minutes)

    Use the activity at the end of each chapter so students practise the idea the way it was first worked out.

Discussion questions that work for any story

  • What problem were people trying to solve?
  • Why do you think some people refused to accept the new idea at first?
  • Which cultures contributed, and how did the idea travel between them?
  • How do we use this idea today?

A note on accuracy

Each story is checked against published histories of mathematics. Where a well-known tale is a legend rather than a recorded fact, the story says so. If you spot an error, please let us know through the About page.
